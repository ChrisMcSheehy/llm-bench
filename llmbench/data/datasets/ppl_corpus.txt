The maintenance of a data platform is less about grand architecture than about the quiet discipline of small, consistent decisions. A column named well today saves an hour of confusion next quarter. A test written when a model is first built catches the silent drift that would otherwise surface only in a board report. Engineers who last are the ones who treat the boring parts as the real work: naming, documenting, validating, and refusing to let a convenient shortcut become tomorrow's outage. Systems do not fail all at once; they erode, one unchecked assumption at a time, until the gap between what the data claims and what the world contains grows too wide to ignore.
